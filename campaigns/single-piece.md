# Archetype: single-piece

```yaml
id: single-piece
version: 1.0.0
status: stable
beats: 5
intent: one garment, deep enough to justify its price
```

## When to use

One piece carries the campaign. Typically a hero garment, a high price point, or
a piece whose construction is the story.

The highest-volume archetype — most reels are single-piece — and the one where
craft evidence does the most work.

---

## Shape

| # | Function | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `establish` | Context. Where this piece belongs. Piece present but not explained |
| 2 | `escalate` | The maker's detail. Macro. Construction, weight, finishing |
| 3 | `rest` | The piece at rest. Unworn, or worn without performance |
| 4 | `turn` | The piece in use. What it does that others do not |
| 5 | `close` | Departure. The piece leaving frame, or the world without it |

**Turn at 4 of 5.**

---

## Required inputs

- `collection-dna` — the piece's `detail` field is load-bearing
- `brand-voice` — product register
- `visual-identity` — `lens` and `light` in full
- Exactly one piece at status `production` or `available`

---

## Rules

1. **Beat 2 is non-negotiable.** The maker's detail is what separates this from a
   product shot. If Collection DNA has no detail for the piece, report the gap —
   do not proceed.
2. **One claim maximum.** A single piece supports a single assertion. Two claims
   halve both.
3. **Never state the price or its justification.** The construction is the
   justification (`premium.md` — price is a consequence).
4. **Beat 3 has no person, or no performance.** Stillness is where weight and
   drape read.
5. **Copy on two beats maximum**, and beat 4 is usually stronger silent.

---

## Common failure

Beat 2 becomes a generic macro shot — fabric texture with no specific detail —
and the campaign reduces to atmosphere around an unremarkable garment.

The test: could beat 2's direction have been written without reading this
piece's Collection DNA entry? If yes, it is decoration, not evidence.
