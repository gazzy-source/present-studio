# Archetype: world-building

```yaml
id: world-building
version: 1.0.0
status: stable
beats: 4
intent: build the world the brand belongs to; product incidental
```

## When to use

Between drops, sustaining presence, or establishing territory before a launch.
The lowest-commercial-intent archetype and the highest for brand accumulation.

Also the highest-volume archetype for reels — cheap to produce, and the primary
engine of motif accumulation.

---

## Shape

| # | Function | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `establish` | Place and hour. No subject, or subject distant |
| 2 | `rest` | Detail of the world. Surface, texture, light on material |
| 3 | `escalate` | Human presence enters. Product incidental, unremarked |
| 4 | `close` | The world continuing without us |

**Turn:** beat 3 functions as the turn in a four-beat sequence. The arrival of a
person *is* the turn — declare it as `turn` in the campaign instance so the
single-turn rule is satisfied.

---

## Required inputs

- `visual-identity` — `environments` and `motifs` are load-bearing
- `campaign-memory` — motif states must be checked; this archetype spends motifs
  fastest
- `collection-dna` — optional
- `brand-voice` — atmospheric register, if any copy at all

---

## Rules

1. **No product claim.** If nothing is claimed, G1 has nothing to check — this is
   the archetype's advantage. Adding a claim forfeits it.
2. **Usually silent.** Zero or one copy beat. This archetype's power is that it
   asks nothing.
3. **Motifs must be declared.** This is where motifs accumulate; improvising here
   corrupts the brand's visual vocabulary fastest (principle 7).
4. **Never set on the `never` list.** Environment exclusions bind absolutely.
5. **Repetition is intended.** Unlike other archetypes, running world-building
   repeatedly is correct — it is how a world becomes recognisable. Vary the
   environment, hold the light.

---

## Common failure

It becomes mood-board filler: attractive, brand-agnostic, forgettable. Fails the
substitution test because nothing in it is specific to the brand.

The test: remove the logo and the caption. Would an existing customer recognise
it? If not, the motifs were decorative rather than declared.
