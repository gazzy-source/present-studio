# Present Clothing — Audience

> **Status: PROPOSED.** No customer data, research or founder description was
> supplied. Derived from the stance in `brand-bible.md`. Replace with real
> segments when available.

Supporting document. Not contract-governed. Supplies the "who" that campaign
briefs assume.

---

## Primary

**People who buy few things and keep them.**

Not defined by age or income. Defined by a purchasing behaviour: they research
before buying, buy infrequently, spend more per item, and expect the item to
outlast the interest in it.

They own a coat they have worn for nine years and will tell you where they got
it.

### What they are actually buying

Not the garment. **Permission to stop looking.**

The relief of a solved problem — an object good enough that the category closes.
This is why range width is a liability for this audience: choice reopens the
question they paid to close.

### How they decide

| They trust | They discount |
|---|---|
| Numbers — weight, dimensions, fibre content | Adjectives |
| Construction detail | Lifestyle imagery |
| The same product available for years | Seasonal newness |
| What the maker chose not to do | Awards, press, endorsement |
| Other owners' long-term wear | Launch-week enthusiasm |

They read specifications before they read copy. Copy that withholds
specifications reads as concealment.

---

## Secondary

**People arriving through the world, not the product.**

Found the brand through atmosphere — the imagery, the weather, the restraint.
They may not buy for a year. They are the audience `world-building` campaigns
serve, and the reason that archetype is repeated without a commercial ask.

Converting them early destroys them. They convert when a specific need arises,
and only if the brand has stayed consistent in the interval.

---

## Who this is not for

Naming the exclusion is required by the stance (`premium.md` — point of view).

- **Trend buyers.** Newness is not offered.
- **People shopping for a future self.** The brand sells for now, in ordinary
  weather.
- **Anyone needing the garment to signal.** No visible branding, no recognisable
  silhouette-as-status.
- **Bargain-driven buyers.** Discount is not part of the model, so the price is
  the price.
- **People who want options.** The range is small on purpose.

---

## What they already believe

Campaigns should assume these and never argue them:

- Cheap clothing is more expensive over time
- Most brands overstate their materials
- Weight and construction correlate with lifespan
- Buying less and better is correct, and mildly difficult to actually do

**Consequence:** the brand never argues for buying-less-better. That argument is
already won; repeating it is condescension. Campaigns show the object that
justifies the belief.

## Objections

| Objection | The honest answer | Never say |
|---|---|---|
| "It costs more than I usually spend" | State the construction and the weight. Stop | "Investment piece" |
| "It's heavier than I expected" | That is the product. Weight is the thesis | Apologise or soften |
| "There isn't much to choose from" | Four pieces is the position, not a limitation | "More coming soon" |
| "I can't tell it apart from cheaper versions" | Show the making (`process` archetype) | Claim superiority |

## Campaign implications

| Archetype | Serves |
|---|---|
| `single-piece` | Primary, at decision point. Specification-dense |
| `process` | Primary, answering the price objection |
| `world-building` | Secondary, long-horizon. No ask |
| `collection-launch` | Both, sparingly — the range is small by design |
| `manifesto` | Secondary, rarely. States the stance that filters the audience |
