## The name

**Present** carries three meanings, and the brand sits where they overlap.

| Meaning | Reading |
|---|---|
| Present *(adj.)* — here, attending | Showing up, on the days it counts and the days it doesn't |
| Presence *(n.)* — physical substance, there-ness | The garment perceptible enough to mark a day |
| Present *(n.)* — a gift, a thing given and kept | The object that outlasts the interest in it |

A person who is present and a garment with presence are the same word. That is
not wordplay; it is the mechanism. An object you can feel is an object that can
keep a record.

## Tier 1 — Brand Belief

**The future is built in the present.**

Permanent. Never deployed as copy. It is the reason the work exists, not the
work.

## Tier 2 — Brand Territory

**The Middle.**

Not the decision. Not the arrival. The interval between them, which is where
nearly all of a life actually happens and which no brand currently occupies.

## Tier 3 — Tension and Promise

**Tension.** The work that builds you is rarely visible while it is happening.

**Promise.** You stop needing to explain yourself.

Both permanent. Both internal. Neither ever appears as copy — the moment the
brand states its own meaning, the reader stops feeling it and starts deciding
whether they agree.

## Tier 4 — Product Philosophy

Every product carries physical evidence that it was made to be used repeatedly.
Weight is the first route to that evidence, not the principle. Perceptibility,
evidence of decision, and continuity are the three requirements; each category
declares its own proof.

Stated in full in `product-philosophy.md`. Per-piece proof in
`collection-dna.md`.

## Stance

**Clothing should be evidence of the days, not a bid on the outcome.**

Rejected: aspiration, the styled life, the garment as a claim on who you might
become. Present Clothing is worn now, in ordinary weather, doing ordinary things
that have not paid off yet.

**Who this alienates:** trend buyers, people shopping for a future self, anyone
who needs the garment to do the talking. Intentional.

---

## What the brand is against

- Newness as a reason to buy
- Clothing designed for photographs rather than wear
- Progress narrated as achievement
- Encouragement — being told you are doing well is evidence the question was open
- Ranges wide enough that nothing in them is definitive

---

## Category position

> `unverified` — requires founder confirmation.

Premium streetwear for gym and street. Small range, repeated across seasons,
revised rather than replaced.

Positioned against elevated basics on one side and scarcity-driven streetwear on
the other. Construction is the proof; the range is deliberately small; the intent
is daily wear.

**Range logic is philosophically load-bearing, not a merchandising preference.**
A garment replaced each season cannot accumulate a record, and a brand that
replaces its range each season cannot participate in what this brand does.

---

## The world

Urban, overcast, unglamorous hours. Concrete, stairwells, car parks, training
rooms before opening, wet stone, unheated rooms with good light, empty streets
before traffic. Working buildings rather than styled interiors.

Never: sunshine, resorts, nightlife, styled apartments, crowds, warmth as an
aesthetic.

---

## Brand behaviours

1. **Repeat rather than replace.** The same piece, revised, for years.
2. **State the number.** gsm, ounces, dimensions. Facts over adjectives.
3. **Never explain the feeling.** Show the hour, the place, the hand.
4. **Sell nothing at speed.** No urgency, no countdowns, no scarcity mechanics.
5. **Show the making.** Craft evidence is the price justification.
6. **Recognition without encouragement.** The brand sees the middle and does not
   comment on it.

## Tagline status

**Unfrozen.** Three candidate signature lines are held open and will be selected
only after a substantial body of work exists:

- The Future Is Built Here.
- Built in the Present.
- The Work Wears In.

No line is deployed until selection. The selected line is a signature used
sparingly, not a slogan locked to every asset.

Blocking Phase 5.

2. Price band
3. Per piece: materials with fibre percentages, weight, construction, sizing,
   price, and the maker's detail
4. Mill and factory names, or an explicit decision to make no origin claims
5. Geography — base city and market, which fixes every campaign location ID
