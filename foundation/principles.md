# Operating Principles

The invariants every engine assumes. These are not style preferences. They are
the reasoning rules that make output premium by construction rather than by
editing.

When an engine faces a choice the contracts do not settle, it resolves it here.

---

## 1. Specificity beats adjectives

"Heavyweight brushed cotton, garment-dyed, seven washes" outperforms "premium
quality fabric" in every context, always.

Adjectives are claims. Nouns are evidence. An adjective asks the audience to
believe; a specific noun lets them verify. Premium brands earn belief by being
checkable.

**Rule:** if an adjective can be replaced by a concrete noun, replace it. If it
cannot, delete it.

---

## 2. Restraint is the signal

What is removed communicates more than what is added. A brand that says three
things is heard. A brand that says nine is skimmed.

Volume signals anxiety. Silence signals certainty. The audience reads the
confidence before they read the words.

**Rule:** every artifact should be cuttable by 30% without losing meaning. If it
is not, it was overwritten.

---

## 3. Never explain the feeling

Naming the emotion destroys it. "This jacket makes you feel powerful" replaces
the audience's experience with an instruction.

Construct the conditions; let the feeling arrive uninvited. Show the empty
street, the weight of the fabric, the hour. The feeling is the audience's to
have.

**Rule:** no artifact states the emotion it intends to produce.

---

## 4. Show the world, not the product

The garment is the artifact. The world is the reason. Audiences buy entry to a
world and accept the garment as the ticket.

Product-first output describes. World-first output invites. Only one of those is
remembered a week later.

**Rule:** lead with context, hour, place, posture, weather, sound. The product
enters as a consequence of the world, never as the subject of the sentence.

---

## 5. Tension makes things memorable

Frictionless work is forgettable. Memorability lives in productive contradiction:
workwear cut for stillness, formality worn carelessly, softness in a hard place.

A campaign without tension is a catalogue.

**Rule:** every campaign carries one named tension. If it cannot be named in a
sentence, it does not exist.

---

## 6. Confidence never persuades

Urgency, superlatives, countdowns and exclamation marks are all the same signal:
this brand is worried you will leave.

Premium brands state and stop. The absence of a sales mechanic *is* the sales
mechanic.

**Rule:** no artifact contains urgency language, superlatives, or a call to
action phrased as pressure.

---

## 7. Consistency compounds

A distinctive move repeated becomes recognition. The same move varied endlessly
stays noise. The brand's palette, cadence, framing and vocabulary should be
narrow enough that a fragment is identifiable without a logo.

**Rule:** prefer repeating an established motif over inventing a new one. New
motifs are added deliberately, not incidentally.

---

## 8. Every claim is traceable

Nothing asserted about a product exists unless it traces to Collection DNA. No
invented materials, provenance, process, or heritage.

Fabrication is not a stylistic failure. It is a factual one, and in apparel it is
a legal one.

**Rule:** if it is not in the Brand Pack, it cannot be said. Report the gap
instead of filling it.

---

## 9. Constraint before generation

Unconstrained generation produces plausible mediocrity. The system's advantage is
that every output is bounded by a contract, a voice, and a DNA before the first
word.

**Rule:** read the constraint, then generate. Never the reverse.

---

## Precedence

When principles conflict, resolve in this order:

1. **Traceability** (8) — accuracy overrides everything
2. **Restraint** (2) — when unsure, cut
3. **Specificity** (1)
4. **Consistency** (7)

Everything else is judgment.
